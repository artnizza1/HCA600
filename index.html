For the instructor: design rationale, competency alignment, and where this sits in the course

Andragogical structure

  • Need to know before content. The case and the stakes arrive before any framework does. Meadows is never named until the student has already used a leverage point.
  • Prior commitment. The learner records an answer before seeing anything, and that answer stays pinned to the accountability bar as a dashed line. Adult learning runs on the gap between what a person already believes and what the evidence shows, so the gap is made literally visible rather than described.
  • Self-direction. The slider moves in both directions with no gating, no score, no correct endpoint, and the prediction step is skippable. The learner sets the pace and can retreat to compare.
  • Experience as the text. The second work question asks for an incident from the learner's own organization mapped onto these nodes. In a cohort of working professionals that is the richest content in the room, and it is theirs.
  • Problem-centered, not subject-centered. The organizing unit is one incident, not a chapter on feedback loops.
  • Immediate application. The final question forces the analysis down to the learner's actual span of control, which protects against the failure mode where systems thinking becomes an excuse to blame the board and do nothing.

The ethical correction at level six

Individual accountability rises slightly from level five to level six rather than continuing to fall. This is deliberate. A tool that runs from "the nurse did it" to "nobody did it" teaches something false, and the sharpest students in the cohort will say so. Level six applies the just culture triage, human error against at-risk behavior against reckless choice, and returns a reframed individual duty. Systems thinking redistributes accountability and makes it specific. It does not dissolve it.

Program competency alignment

  • #8 Systems Thinking and Innovation, foundational awareness. Analyzing interdependencies is the entire mechanic. The reinforcing loops close on screen at levels four and five.
  • #3 Healthcare Operations Excellence, developing. Levels two and three are process design, staffing models, and human factors in procurement.
  • #1 Values-Based Leadership. Level six is an ethics problem in the shape of an interface, and psychological safety is the mechanism in loop R1.
  • #7 Professional Excellence and Communication. The export produces a structured written analysis for the discussion board.

Placement

  • Week 3, continuous improvement and high reliability. Best single fit. Loop R1 is the HRO reporting culture argument made visible, and root cause analysis is what levels one through three are doing badly and then well.
  • Week 4, governance and clinical excellence. Level five is the board's causal role, which is the hardest thing to make graduate students feel rather than recite.
  • Week 5, nursing and human resources. The learning outcome for that week already names feedback loops driving burnout and retention. Loop R2 is that loop.

Suggested discussion prompt

Move the slider to the level at which you believe a competent chief operating officer should be operating, then defend that choice against a peer who chose a different level. Do not argue that a deeper level is automatically better. Argue what is gained and what is lost.
